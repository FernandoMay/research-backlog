## Title
"Cognitive Offloading, Autonomy, and Human-AI Trust in Agentic Systems"

## Proposal (~150 words)

This chapter offers a conceptual analysis of decision delegation in autonomous multi-agent systems, focusing on how cognitive offloading to AI agents reshapes human autonomy and trust. As agentic systems increasingly take initiative in planning and execution, the boundary between assistance and substitution becomes blurred. We examine the trade-off between efficiency gains and potential erosion of human agency, considering alignment challenges and the psychological dynamics of automation trust bias (including over-reliance, under-reliance, and calibration failures). Drawing from literature in cognitive psychology, human factors, and human-AI interaction, we identify conditions under which offloading preserves meaningful human control versus those that risk diminishing autonomy. The analysis emphasizes actionable principles for designing agentic systems that support cognitive work while maintaining human oversight and agency. By clarifying these dynamics, this chapter aims to inform system designers and researchers working at the intersection of AI and psychology.
