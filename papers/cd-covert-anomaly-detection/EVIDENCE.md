## The research question (read from the code, not the title)

> Given one 20 480-sample complex snapshot at 20 MHz containing AWGN, primary users, impulsive
> bursts, and an optional covert BPSK tone, can a detector trained **only** on normal
> observations separate the covert observations, and does fusing scores across K sensors help?

The mechanism is **total-power discrimination**. The covert centre frequency is redrawn uniformly
from (2.3, 5.9) MHz *per observation*, so no fixed subband or fixed spectral statistic can localise
it. The only class-consistent learnable feature is aggregate received power.

## The three inherited discrepancies, verified independently

The earlier audit reported three. I checked each against the artifacts rather than inheriting it.
**All three are confirmed, but one of them is materially misdiagnosed** — see #2.

### 1. Isolation Forest `0.999` vs `1.000` — **CONFIRMED**

`figures/metrics.json` → `mc.IForest.aucs` is `[1.0, 1.0, ... ]` × 20. All twenty seeds exactly 1.0.
`sd = 0.0000`, `Pd = 1.0000`, `Pfa = 0.0000`, all 20 seeds. The abstract's 0.999 is simply wrong and
the body's 1.000 is right. Resolved in favour of the artifact.

### 2. Cyclic-stationary "AUC 0.600" vs `0.6266` / `0.6367` — **CONFIRMED AS A DISAGREEMENT, BUT THE PRIOR FRAMING IS WRONG**

The earlier audit treated 0.600 as an unsupported number. It is not. I re-ran the exact call and got:

```
run_all_methods(env, n_train=250, n_test_normal=250, n_test_covert=200, covert_power_db=-85)
  -> Cyclostationary AUC = 0.599920
```

That is a real, reproducible run of committed code, and it is the value printed in the legend of the
committed `fig_roc_curves.pdf` (verified by extracting the PDF's text content stream: the legend reads
`Cyclostationary (AUC=0.600)`).

So 0.600 is **correct for the 250/250/200 configuration** and is *not* an error. What **is** an error
is much worse and was missed:

- The 20-seed mean at 100/100/80 is **0.6367**; the paired sweep point is **0.626625**.
- The same section that reports Cyclo 0.600 reports Energy 0.989, AE 0.943 and OC-SVM 0.999 — and
  **those three are MC trial 1**, not the 250/250/200 run. The actual 250/250/200 values are
  **1.000, 1.000, 1.000, 1.000, 0.600**.
- So §VI-B is a **Frankenstein**: four numbers from the sweep/MC-trial-1 run, one number (Cyclic 0.600)
  from the 250/250/200 run — and the figure it cites was drawn from the latter, so its legend
  contradicts its own text on four of five detectors.

**The real finding is the internal contradiction, not the 0.600.**

### 3. "−30 dB window" — **CONFIRMED, with precise bounds**

Recomputed from `metrics.json.sweep` as (lowest power with AUC ≥ 0.99) − (lowest power with AUC ≥ 0.5):

| Detector | chance from | reaches 0.99 at | width |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isolation Forest | −110 dBm | −95 dBm | **15 dB** |
| One-Class SVM | −120 dBm | −85 dBm | **35 dB** |
| Energy | −120 dBm | −80 dBm | **40 dB** |
| PCA autoencoder | −100 dBm | **never** (max 0.9659) | — |
| Cyclic proxy | −90 dBm | **never** (max 0.7647) | — |

The prior audit's "15 dB (IForest) to 40 dB (Energy), with two detectors never reaching 0.99" is
**exactly right**.

## Fatal findings

These are the ones the earlier audit did not report. Each is verified by execution, not by reading.

### F1. The benchmark is saturated, so the detector ranking is not recoverable

`IForest − OC-SVM`, paired per seed: `[0.00038, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0.01, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0.02, 0, 0, 0]`
→ mean `+0.00152`, sd `0.00489`, **exactly zero in 17 of 20 trials**.
Against Energy: **exactly zero in 16 of 20**.

Four of five detectors exceed 0.977; IForest is perfect in 20/20. A metric pinned at its ceiling with
tied per-seed differences cannot support "Isolation Forest is best". The paper reports no confidence
intervals and no tests anywhere.

### F2. The primary users are spectrally flat, not narrowband — this is why the benchmark is easy

Paper §II: primary users are "OFDM subcarriers at fixed centre frequencies of 2.4, 2.6, 5.2, 5.8 MHz".
Code line 90–91:

```python
phases  = self.rng.uniform(0, 2*np.pi, n)          # a NEW random phase for EVERY sample
pri_sig = np.sqrt(pw) * np.exp(1j * (2*np.pi*fp*1e6*t + phases))
```

A tone with a per-sample random phase is **white noise at the PU's power**. Measured: averaging the
periodogram over 40 realisations, max/median bin power = **1.496** — consistent with a flat spectrum.
Four real narrowband emitters would give a contrast of one to two orders of magnitude.

The covert signal *is* narrowband (measured H1/H0 median power ratio **3.41** inside (2.3, 5.9) MHz vs
**1.05** outside, peak 8.21) — but because its frequency is redrawn per observation, the excess moves
and no fixed feature can use it. The learnable evidence is total power, which is exactly what the
energy baseline reads. **This is why the energy detector reaches 0.9988 and why the metric saturates.**

The paper's whole spectral argument ("the covert signal's localized power contribution to the
spectrum") describes a spectrum the simulator never produces.

### F3. The feature-importance figure is fabricated

`cd_simulator.py:634-636`:

```python
pseudo_y = np.zeros(len(X_train));  pseudo_y[len(X_train)//2:] = 1
f_scores, p_vals = f_classif(X_train[:len(pseudo_y)], pseudo_y)
```

`X_train` is 50 **all-normal** observations. The "labels" are a positional split of normal data.
The ANOVA measures **normal vs normal**, not normal vs covert.

Worse: `ac_0` is the autocorrelation at lag 0 normalised by itself, and `ac_max` is the max over lags
*including* lag 0. Both are **identically 1.0**, confirmed by measurement (min = max = 1.000000000000).
`f_classif` returns `NaN` for a constant feature, and `np.argsort(...)[::-1]` puts `NaN` first — so the
committed figure's **top two bars are two zero-information constants**. The remaining eight have
p-values 0.023–0.19 over 41 tests, i.e. nothing survives multiple-comparison correction.

The paper's textual reading of this figure (naming psd_max, band 2/3, psd_p95) matches neither the
figure's axis labels nor anything in the code. **This figure is excluded from the rewrite.**

### F4. The C reference kernel is memory-unsafe, aborts, and prints a self-contradictory ROC

```c
#define N_FEATURES 41
```

`extract_features` writes **42** values. AddressSanitizer:

```
ERROR: AddressSanitizer: heap-buffer-overflow ... WRITE of size 4
    #0 extract_features cd_kernel.c:281
0x629000009210 is located 0 bytes after 16400-byte region  (100 * 41 * 4)
```

Verified directly: the overflow writes sample *i*'s slot 41 into **sample i+1's `psd_mean`**, corrupting
the training matrix used to set the energy threshold. Every execution terminates with `SIGABRT`,
exit status **134**.

The committed binary prints, at −85, −92 and −100 dBm:

```
AUC = 0.0000
Pd @ best operating point = 1.0000
Pfa @ best operating point = 0.0000
```

An AUC of 0 with perfect separation is arithmetically impossible for a correct ROC. A reimplementation
of the same `main()` routine with a correctly sized buffer returns `1.0000`, so this is a defect of the
committed binary, not a property of the method. `compute_auc` is itself correct (unit-tested in
isolation: perfect separation → 1.0000, inverted → 0.0000).

Additional kernel defects: seeds from `time(0)` (not reproducible); `ac_max` hard-coded to 0; no STFT;
a Hann window (contradicting the Python, which has none); and the "500 kHz BPSK" covert signal draws a
fresh sign per sample inside the sample loop, making it a **full-bandwidth random sequence**, not BPSK.
It is not a reference implementation of the Python pipeline. **Excluded from all results.**

### F5. The sensor-scalability figure has no artifact, and its claims are false

Confirmed: `plot_sensor_scalability()` computes `aucs` and discards them; `main()` saves only
`{'mc': ..., 'sweep': ...}`. `metrics.json` has no K-indexed series. The earlier audit's finding is
**correct**.

I regenerated it with the committed code (`scripts/recover.py` → `data/recovered_scalability.json`).
The recovered values, at n = 80/80/60, K = 1/2/4/8:

| Detector | K=1 | K=2 | K=4 | K=8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Energy | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| PCA autoencoder | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | **0.9502** |
| One-Class SVM | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| Isolation Forest | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| Cyclic proxy | 0.6500 | **0.6360** | 0.7435 | 0.6990 |

**A single sensor already saturates every strong detector.** There is no fusion gain to report. The
committed figure's y-axis spans 0.65–1.00, consistent with the recovered data and inconsistent with the
paper's claimed 0.55 start.

Methodological caveat, stated in the rewrite: the upstream routine advances **one** `env_base` across
all 20 cells, so each (K, method) cell is a different dataset and the K-trend is confounded with the
data draw. n = 1 per cell, no dispersion, unpaired. This is the h266 pseudo-replication pattern.

### F6. Pseudo-replication in the power sweep

`snr_sweep()` re-creates `SpectrumEnvironment(seed=SEED)` at every power. Because the covert power
changes no random draw, all nine points share **identical** noise and primary-user realisations. The
sweep is therefore *paired*, not independent, and its −85 dBm point is bit-identical to MC trial 1.
The paper presents the sweep as a nine-point result with no dispersion and no mention of either fact.

## Statistical validity

| Property | Assessment |
|---|---|
| 20 independent replications in the MC? | **Yes.** Derived seeds, genuinely different data. |
| Dispersion reported upstream? | Only a mean and a population SD in one table. |
| Dispersion reported here? | Yes — sample SD, min, 95 % CI, and a ceiling count. |
| Any significance test? | **None**, upstream or here. A test would be uninformative: the IForest−OCSVM difference is exactly 0 in 17/20 trials. |
| Pseudo-replication in the MC? | No. |
| Pseudo-replication elsewhere? | **Yes** — the sensor sweep (unpaired, chained) and the power sweep (a duplicated point, n=1 elsewhere). |
| Anything compared without dispersion? | The entire sensor-scaling claim, and the entire "ML beats Energy by 5–10 dB" claim. |
| Ceiling effect? | **Yes, fatal.** IForest sd = 0.0000 across 20 trials. |

**Verdict on the 20-seed run:** the *experiment* is sound and honestly reported. The *metric* is
saturated, so it cannot support a detector ranking. It can support a ceiling finding, which is what
the rewrite reports.

## Sensor-scalability figure: artifact behind it?

**No.** The generator exists (`plot_sensor_scalability`) and the committed PNG/PDF were produced by it,
but the underlying series was never persisted anywhere. `metrics.json` contains only `mc` and `sweep`.
This is a *reproducibility* failure rather than a fabrication: the figure is not hand-drawn, and
re-running the committed code regenerates it. But the **claims made about it in the paper are false**,
which is worse. The series has now been regenerated and committed here as
`data/recovered_scalability.json`.

## Baselines: what the paper actually compares

Exactly one comparison: five off-the-shelf one-class methods, all applied to the same self-generated
feature set, with a total-power threshold defined in the same codebase as the only baseline.
**Zero of the 10 cited references is compared numerically.**

A reviewer would demand:

1. At least two **published** spectrum-anomaly or covert-detection methods on the identical generator and seeds.
2. **Cooperative spectrum sensing** baselines with AND / OR / $m$-out-of-$K$ voting — the package implements
   `hard_and`, `hard_or` and `hard_k` in `DistributedFusion` and **evaluates none of them**.
3. A **matched-filter / square-law** LPI baseline, which is the standard covert-detection reference.
4. A **correctly implemented** cyclostationary detector, which the package's fifth detector is not (F4/F18).

## Minimum experiment set to support a positive paper

1. Fix the operating point in advance (e.g. require the energy baseline to sit near chance) — do not
   choose the point that separates the candidates.
2. Replace the per-sample-random-phase primary user with a genuinely narrowband or OFDM model.
3. Add the baseline set in item 1–4 above, on identical seeds.
4. Replicate the power sweep with ≥ 20 seeds per power and report dispersion, not a single paired curve.
5. Make the sensor sweep **nested**: the K=8 dataset must contain the K=4 dataset, so the only difference
   is sensor count.
6. Implement a real cyclostationary detector, and compute feature importance against true labels.
7. Fix the C kernel's buffer overrun, seed it deterministically, and either make it a true reference
   implementation of the Python pipeline or delete it.
8. Fix the periodogram (window it, and use the whole observation rather than 2.5 % of it).
